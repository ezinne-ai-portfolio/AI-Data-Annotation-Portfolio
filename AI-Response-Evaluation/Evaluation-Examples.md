# Practical AI Response Evaluation Examples

## Introduction

This document presents selected exercises completed during my independent AI response evaluation training.

The exercises helped me practise comparing AI-generated responses, applying evaluation criteria, identifying misleading information and writing evidence-based justifications.

These are learning exercises, not paid client projects.

---

## Example 1: Preference Ranking – Malaria Information

**Task:** Compare two AI-generated responses and select the more accurate and helpful response.

**Response A:** Claimed that drinking water cures malaria and can replace medication.

**Response B:** Explained that drinking water does not cure malaria, although it can help prevent dehydration. It also indicated that suspected malaria requires testing and appropriate treatment.

**My decision:** Preferred Response B.

**Reasoning:** Response A contained misleading medical information. Response B provided a safer and more accurate explanation.

**Skills demonstrated:**
- Preference ranking
- Factual accuracy assessment
- Medical misinformation identification
- Safety evaluation
- Written justification

---

## Example 2: Rubric-Based Evaluation – Diabetes Information

**Task:** Evaluate two AI-generated responses using five quality criteria.

| Criterion | Response A | Response B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accuracy | 1/5 | 5/5 |
| Relevance | 4/5 | 5/5 |
| Completeness | 1/5 | 5/5 |
| Safety | 1/5 | 5/5 |
| Clarity | 4/5 | 5/5 |

**My assessment:** Response B achieved higher scores across all five evaluation criteria.

**Reasoning:** Using a consistent scoring rubric made it possible to compare the responses systematically rather than relying on personal preference.

**Skills demonstrated:**
- Rubric-based evaluation
- Quality assessment
- Response comparison
- Structured decision-making

## Example 3: Fact-Checking and Source Verification

**Task:** Examine AI-generated information attributed to the World Health Organization (WHO).

The exercise involved checking claims about a publication date and a malaria statistic.

**My approach:**
- Identify the factual claims.
- Recognise the need to verify the publication date.
- Check whether the statistic is supported by the cited source.
- Avoid accepting an AI-generated citation without verification.

**Skills demonstrated:**
- Fact-checking
- Source verification
- Critical thinking
- Detection of potentially unsupported claims

## Example 4: Hallucination and Medical Information Detection

**Task:** Identify misleading or incorrect medical statements in an AI-generated response.

**Exercise result:** 8/10.

During the exercise, I correctly identified an incorrect claim about antibiotics. I also learned that my assessment of ACTs (artemisinin-based combination therapies) needed improvement.

**Learning outcome:** AI evaluation requires careful checking of technical information. A statement should not be marked incorrect simply because it is unfamiliar.

**Skills demonstrated:**
- Misinformation detection
- Critical evaluation
- Medical information awareness
- Learning from evaluation errors
